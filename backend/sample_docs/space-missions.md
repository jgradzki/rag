# A Short History of Robotic Mars Exploration

## Early missions

Mariner 4, launched by NASA in November 1964, performed the first successful flyby of Mars on July 14, 1965. It returned 21 photographs showing a cratered, Moon-like surface, which ended popular hopes of finding canals or vegetation.

The Soviet Mars 3 lander became the first spacecraft to achieve a soft landing on Mars in December 1971, but it stopped transmitting after about 20 seconds, most likely because of a dust storm.

Viking 1 and Viking 2 (NASA, 1976) were the first fully successful landers. Each carried a biology package that tested soil samples for signs of microbial life. The results were ambiguous and are still debated today.

## The rover era

- **Sojourner** (Mars Pathfinder, 1997) was the first wheeled rover on Mars. It weighed only 10.6 kg and travelled about 100 metres in total.
- **Spirit and Opportunity** (2004) were twin rovers designed for a 90-day mission. Spirit worked until 2010; Opportunity operated for almost 15 years, until a planet-wide dust storm in 2018, and drove 45.16 km — a record for off-Earth driving at the time.
- **Curiosity** (2012) is a car-sized rover powered by a radioisotope thermoelectric generator, so it does not depend on sunlight. It landed in Gale Crater using the "sky crane" manoeuvre and found evidence of an ancient lake environment that could have supported microbial life.
- **Perseverance** (2021) landed in Jezero Crater, a former river delta. It collects rock core samples in sealed tubes for a future sample-return mission. It brought the Ingenuity helicopter, which made the first powered, controlled flight on another planet on April 19, 2021, and eventually completed 72 flights.
- **Zhurong** (China, 2021) made China the second country to operate a rover on Mars.

## Key lessons

Dust is the main enemy of solar-powered missions: it covers solar panels and blocks sunlight during storms. Landing is the riskiest phase — the thin atmosphere is too thick to ignore but too thin for parachutes alone, so missions combine heat shields, parachutes, and retro-rockets.
